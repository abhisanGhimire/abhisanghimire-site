02 · Publications

Published research

Preprint

Social Determinants of Health and Fentanyl Overdose Mortality Across US Counties: An XGBoost and SHAP Analysis Identifying Silent Risk Counties and Treatment Deserts

Kabi Raj Tiruwa, Abhisan Ghimire, Anuj Kumar Shah · arXiv:2605.08230 · 2026

A county-level machine learning study combining CDC WONDER mortality data, the Social Vulnerability Index, health behavior data, and healthcare workforce files across 975 U.S. counties. An XGBoost model with SHAP interpretation identified disability rate, hypertension, smoking, and lack of vehicle access as the strongest predictors of fentanyl overdose mortality, correctly flagging 71% of high-risk counties (R² = 0.457). The analysis surfaced 143 "silent risk counties" with elevated structural vulnerability but not-yet-elevated death counts, and found treatment-desert counties had 52.6% higher mortality, pointing to where prevention investment matters most.

Preprint

Wearable-Grade Lead Reduction Disproportionately Degrades ECG AI Performance in Elderly Patients: Evidence from PTB-XL and MIT-BIH

Kabi Raj Tiruwa, Abhisan Ghimire · medRxiv · June 2026

A 1D residual neural network trained on 21,091 PTB-XL recordings shows that reducing clinical 12-lead ECGs down to a single wearable-grade lead degrades AI diagnostic accuracy far more for older patients than younger ones: a 14.1 percentage-point drop for patients 75+ versus just 0.4 points for patients under 40, a roughly 40-fold gap (p < 0.0001), with external validation confirming the effect held across datasets. The findings argue for age-stratified performance reporting as a minimum standard in wearable AI-ECG validation and regulatory review.

Publication

Understanding Hate Group Videos on YouTube

Shuo Niu, Keegan Veazey, Phoenix Pagan, Abhisan Ghimire · ACM CSCW'22 Companion · pp. 32–36 · November 2022

A preliminary grounded-theory analysis of 96 YouTube videos from U.S. hate groups, using open-coding and affinity diagramming to identify common video styles, and annotating each video's diagnostic, prognostic, and motivational framing to understand how these groups promote collective action. Presented in the CSCW'22 companion track. My contribution centered on data collection and Python-based research tooling supporting the analysis.

08 · About

A software engineer and educator pursuing doctoral research

I'm a software engineer by training and practice, and an educator by current role, and I'm looking to bring both into a PhD. Teaching has shown me, in a way that shipping code alone never did, how people actually learn, adopt, and get stuck on the systems we build, and that gap between what we design and how people really use it is the question I want to study formally.

I hold an M.S. in Computer Science from Clark University (2023, GPA 3.97/4.00, Alpha Epsilon Lambda Honor Society) and a B.E. in Computer Science from Dr. Ambedkar Institute of Technology (2019). Most of my career has been hands-on software and web development: building GIS-based infrastructure management, COVID-19 monitoring, and disaster-risk assessment systems in Laravel, OpenLayers, GeoServer, and PostgreSQL for a national telecom regulator and public health partners.

At Clark University, I also contributed research tooling to a published study of hate group videos on YouTube (ACM CSCW'22), and more recently co-authored applied machine learning research spanning public health and clinical AI. I currently work as an Education Coordinator at the Boys & Girls Club of Worcester, teaching programming, web development, and computational thinking to youth, work that has taught me as much about how people learn as any of my formal training did.

That combination, production software engineering, applied research tooling (Python, Selenium, OpenCV, XGBoost), and years spent watching beginners learn to build things, is what I want to bring to doctoral research at the intersection of software systems and human behavior.
